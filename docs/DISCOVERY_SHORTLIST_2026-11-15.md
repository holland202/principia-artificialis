## Ranked candidates

All costs below are **design counts**, printed in `qualification.txt`, not measured runtime or
power estimates. Small screens can reject feasibility or a strong prediction. A positive screen
does not establish novelty, statistical confirmation, or independent reproduction.

### 1. Source-specific instruction reverses its effect across lineage depths

**Exact candidate.** On a pinned model, a tracing directive reduces matched-pair correctness
on direct-source cases and increases it on indirect-source cases, after matching graph record
count, counterbalancing label codes and order, and comparing a generic-carefulness directive.
This is a model/output phenomenon, not a claim about internal thought or persistent learning.

**Why first.** Archived real Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct Q4_K_M runs already supply a directional anomaly.
No training is needed. The [development replay](../results/cst_mech_qual/archive_replay.txt) prints
the following with the original scorer, on previously public data only.

| Archive | No exposure D | Explicit tracing E |
|---|---:|---:|
| Development, one hop | 26/32 | 14/32 |
| Development, two hops | 0/32 | 12/32 |
| Fresh development generation, two hops | 0/32 | 10/32 |

The demonstration arm A scored 0/32 in the second development archive. These data support
investigating an instruction effect, not observed-procedure transfer. The second archive tests
only the deep side, so it does not reproduce the crossover. Original hop groups also used
different generated scenarios. Reply-length associations are post-treatment observations.

**Prior art and residual.** Liu et al. already establish CoT harms in some tasks [S1]. Gao et al.
directly study restated evidence, source declarations, counting instructions, matched placebo
paragraphs, relay protocols, and reasoning settings [S2]. Huryn tests source-marker training,
source cues, distractor evidence, and provenance reasoning [S3]. The inspected sections do not
establish this particular controlled shallow-harm/deep-benefit crossover. That is a **search
boundary**, not proof that nobody has reported it. A different model or binary endpoint alone
would be a replication, not a discovery.

**Cheapest discriminating experiment.** First use a fresh public development screen of **192**
responses: 16 scenario blocks × two depths × shared/independent roots × N/T/G instructions,
all with the same long-output allowance. Precede it with **18** noise responses: two fixed
qualification prompts × three instructions × three repeats. Include direct root-ID/equality
positive controls, constant-label scorer negatives, exact root oracle, both report orders,
and counterbalanced answer codes. Control counts must be fixed before execution, not added
after seeing a promising result. No such model screen was run here.

**Stop or revise.** Stop if the assay cannot resolve roots, the instruction shifts only the
default answer, the shallow harm disappears when intermediate records and length are matched,
or generic carefulness reproduces the whole effect. A floor/ceiling or malformed-output failure
is an assay failure, not evidence against the scientific mechanism. A small null screen is
inconclusive unless its precision excludes the prespecified meaningful effect.

**Decisive follow-up.** Subject to owner approval, preregister a new held-out long-format
experiment with **768** responses: 64 new blocks × two depths × two truths × three instructions.
Require directional evidence at each depth, not just a positive interaction. Freeze meaningful
effect margins, uncertainty intervals, cluster structure and sample-size justification first.
Counterbalancing templates does not create independent template populations. Infer only to
the fixed generator unless independently sampled template families support a broader claim.
An optional short-format diagnostic adds **512** responses and must be separately designated
before execution. A second operator must run the frozen model and independently score fresh
outputs. Archive replay alone does not fulfill that step.

### 2. Fragment redundancy predicts deletion tolerance better than corruption tolerance after severity matching

**Exact candidate.** A pre-fault fragment-information statistic has different predictive value
for deletion and sign corruption even after matching the perturbed layer, activation-change
energy, clean accuracy, output margin and simple weight/activation summaries. This narrows
note040's lost-versus-lying interpretation to a measurable residual.

**Why retained.** Note040 preserves a failed transfer prediction. Its code deletes hidden
activations in one arm and flips input-layer weights in the other. Those interventions differ
in site and magnitude, so the narrative explanation has not been isolated. Note045's failed
anti-vacuity/unlearning predictions further discourage promoting redundancy as a general metric.

**Prior art and residual.** Information-ordered ablation and redundancy/synergy analysis already
exist [S4]. Information-theoretic fault containment in recurrent nets is also published [S5].
The proposed matched-severity residual is unverified, with **low-to-moderate novelty confidence**.
Calling it Quantum Darwinism adds no evidence. A real trained MLP result remains an MLP result,
not radiation validation or evidence about LLM knowledge.

**Cheapest discriminating experiment.** Train **24** small networks: three training regimes ×
four fresh seeds × two fixed nontrivial classification tasks. Split calibration examples,
fault masks, and scoring examples before training. Calibrate deletion and sign corruption on
the same layer to matched activation-change energy using calibration data only. Compare the
fragment metric against norm/margin predictors and a shuffled metric on untouched examples
and masks. Score exact predictions with a separately written forward-pass evaluator. Preserve
every trained model, including failures to reach the qualification accuracy.

**Falsifier.** If the differential prediction disappears after matching intervention site and
severity, the mechanistic explanation is unsupported. If norms/margins predict as well on
held-out seeds, the expensive information statistic has no demonstrated added value. This
screen cannot supply a reliable cross-architecture correlation estimate. A surviving candidate
needs a separately powered, preregistered seed/task holdout and blind reproduction.

### 3. Shared implementation context erases some benefit of model diversity on boundary defects

**Exact candidate.** Given the same unambiguous contract, exposing otherwise isolated coding
models to a common illustrative implementation increases *joint, identical boundary failures*
relative to contract-only generation, beyond changes in their marginal error rates. The causal
factor is shared example exposure. It is not simply same-family versus different-family code.

**Why third.** Note076 already records exposure to another port and a corrected account of
cross-family checks. That creates a concrete confound to isolate, but no causal result yet.
Nogueira et al. directly evaluate failure independence in generated code across models,
languages and prompt strategies [S6]. Kim et al. show correlated LM errors more generally [S7].
The broad idea is already covered. This narrow exposure interaction has **low novelty confidence**
and should be dropped if closer program-synthesis/context-anchoring work settles it.

**Cheapest discriminating experiment.** **128** generated programs: 16 new tiny contracts ×
two model families × two exposure conditions × two independent draws. Use one fixed language,
isolated contexts, matched total input/output budgets, and an external exhaustive finite-domain
oracle with predeclared boundary classes. The common example passes all displayed examples but
contains one predeclared off-example semantic trap; call it a planted exposure, not a naturally
occurring defect. Contract-only receives an equal-length unrelated example. Add a correct-example
qualification control before the study, with its count fixed in the eventual registration.
Do not provide hidden oracle feedback or repair iterations to the models.

**Falsifier.** No increase in joint errors beyond the product of marginal rates, or the same
change under unrelated/correct-example controls, defeats the proposed common-context account.
The planted-defect study could demonstrate susceptibility only. It would not estimate prevalence
in ordinary collaborations. Confirmation needs untouched contracts and an independently authored
oracle, with pairing by contract and multiplicity handled prospectively.

## PR #152 review and developed correction

The document's opening claim is a crossover. Its P1 tests a positive interaction. These differ.
The [registered qualification](CST_MECH_QUAL_001_PREREG.md) constructs this exact counterexample.

| Constructed fixture | Shallow instruction effect | Deep instruction effect | Interaction | Proposed #152 gate | Directional gate |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|---|
| Both depths benefit | 1/4 | 1 | 3/4 | true | false |

The interaction has 48 positive blocks and 16 ties, with exact one-sided sign tail
`1/281474976710656`. This arithmetic says nothing about a model. It shows that #152 can label
P1 supported while its stated same-direction-improvement killer is present.

The package proposes separate shallow-harm and deep-benefit checks, plus an explicit generic-arm
contrast. Effect margins are provisional, not frozen scientific thresholds. The sign test is not
automatically an interval or test for the mean interaction. Shared templates and dependent runs
must not be counted as independent experimental units.

Other required revisions before a campaign:

- Specify whether the claim is merely a crossover or a source-specific crossover. If specificity
  is claimed, an equal generic-carefulness effect cannot be relegated to a harmless secondary result.
- Freeze a matched graph intervention. Original depth changes also changed wording, length and
  scenario identity. The new qualification graph keeps record count and identifier width constant.
- Declare output format as its own intervention. A one-token restriction also changes prompting,
  decoding and external scratchpad access. Longer replies do not measure hidden reasoning.
- Make the neutral/rival prompts tokenizer-matched only after the tokenizer is frozen. Matching
  length does not make a directive semantically inert.
- Publish per-truth confusion counts and label-mapping sensitivity. A general shift toward
  CONFIRMED can masquerade as solving more deep cases while harming shallow shared-root cases.
- Replace the instruction to compare against old sealed prompts with a custodian-held fingerprint
  comparison. We must not open the original holdout to prove the new prompts are disjoint.
- Freeze exact parser, retry policy and exhaustive archive keys. The original archive analyzer can
  overwrite duplicate keys; this package rejects duplicates instead. No old published scorer changed.
