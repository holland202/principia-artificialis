# Licensing and reuse — Principia Artificialis

**Status:** Documentation clarification proposed for owner review (2026-10-19). This page does **not** amend the repository's `LICENSE`, relicense existing works, transfer copyright, or create a new permission requirement for using MIT-licensed material.

## Existing public grant

The repository's root [LICENSE](LICENSE) is the **MIT License**, with the 2026 copyright notice naming Chad Holland. The existing [CITATION.cff](CITATION.cff) also identifies MIT. For material actually covered by that grant, MIT permits use, modification, distribution, private use and commercial use, subject to retaining applicable copyright and license notices. A new explanatory page does not withdraw rights already granted in earlier copies.

The repository includes **software, research text, illustrations, external submissions and citations**. Copyright and grant authority must be checked for individual externally authored artifacts; the root license should not be interpreted as proof that the project owns every contribution or can relicense it. Some files may have separate notices or documented publication permissions. **Do not invent an exception, nor assert a separate license, for an existing work without checking its source and rights.** Unclear rights require review before reuse beyond the applicable existing permissions.

## Forward-looking handling by material type

| Material | Default or proposed handling | What this means |
|---|---|---|
| Existing repository software and accompanying material covered by MIT | **Keep existing MIT terms** | No retroactive restriction or license swap. |
| New code and reference implementations offered for inclusion | **MIT-compatible contribution, with contributor's authority confirmed** | No copyright assignment by default. A distinct license needs review before merge. |
| New research notes, papers or educational text | **Check authorship and grant before publishing/relicensing** | A separately marked Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 option may be considered for **future** author-approved standalone writing; no blanket CC BY grant is made here. |
| Independent external reviews, third-party reports or provided artifacts | **Use only under their existing license or documented author permission** | Permission to host a review or credit an author is not automatically a right to relicense or commercially sublicense their independent work. |
| Third-party datasets, images, references, model weights and dependencies | **Follow each upstream grant** | Maintain provenance and notices; do not assume the root MIT license overrides upstream obligations. |
| PA name, logo and endorsement claims | See [BRAND_POLICY.md](BRAND_POLICY.md) | Brand identity, accurate affiliation and marks are considered separately from software copyright; no new restriction on MIT rights is implied. |
| Future paid products, hosting, integrations or unreleased proprietary code | **Separate commercial arrangement if pursued** | No new proprietary terms apply to existing MIT-licensed releases. |

Creative Commons recommends against CC licenses for software code; consult [Creative Commons FAQ](https://creativecommons.org/faq/) before offering one for separately authored writing. An individual researcher may wish to publish a standalone paper under different terms; this repository does not silently grant that choice on their behalf.

## Contributions, ownership and publication

See [CONTRIBUTOR_RIGHTS.md](CONTRIBUTOR_RIGHTS.md) and [CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md).

- Contributors generally retain copyright in work they author, except where a distinct and valid written arrangement applies. No copyright assignment or broad relicensing authority is requested by this page.
- For ordinary code contributions proposed for the MIT repository, confirm that the submitter is able to offer the work on the stated terms. If a contributor cannot, discuss a separate path **before** accepting it.
- For an independent authored review, record exact author credit and the intended publication scope, and retain the relevant evidence of permission. A public GitHub issue is not automatically consent to republish its text as a separate journal article, marketing testimonial or commercial dataset.
- Always preserve negative results and author independence. Contributor inclusion is neither endorsement nor evidence of scientific validation.
- Participation is voluntary, unpaid and at the participant's pace; no membership fee or required hours. Any paid work must be negotiated separately in writing.

## Commercial and citation clarification

MIT allows someone else to use or commercialize covered public code while preserving its notices. PA may independently offer consulting, integrations, hosting and **separately developed** products under different terms if it holds the necessary rights; it cannot retroactively impose those terms on copies previously distributed under MIT.

Academic and research citation is requested in [CITATION.cff](CITATION.cff); it is distinct from the mandatory MIT copyright/license notice preservation. Neither citation requests nor a brand policy change the MIT text.
